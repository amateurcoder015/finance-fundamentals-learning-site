# Time Value of Money (TVM)

The **Time Value of Money (TVM)** is the foundational bedrock of finance, valuation, corporate capital budgeting, and investment management. At its core, TVM states that a dollar received today is worth **more** than a dollar received in the future.

This core premise holds true because money available today can be invested to earn interest or investment returns, increasing its nominal value over time.

---

## 1. Core Variables & Concepts

When solving TVM problems, we work with five fundamental variables:

- **$PV$ (Present Value)**: The current value of a future sum of money or stream of cash flows discounted at a specific interest rate.
- **$FV$ (Future Value)**: The value of a current asset or cash flow at a specified date in the future based on an assumed rate of growth.
- **$r$ (Interest Rate / Discount Rate)**: The annual interest rate or required rate of return per period.
- **$n$ (Number of Periods)**: The total number of compounding or discounting time periods.
- **$PMT$ (Payment)**: Equal, periodic cash payments made or received each period.

---

## 2. Fundamental TVM Formulas

### Single Cash Flows

- **Future Value of a Single Cash Flow**:
  $$FV = PV \times (1 + r)^n$$

- **Present Value of a Single Cash Flow**:
  $$PV = \frac{FV}{(1 + r)^n}$$

### Compounding Frequency
When interest compounds $m$ times per year (e.g. monthly where $m = 12$, semi-annually where $m = 2$), the periodic interest rate becomes $r / m$ and the total number of periods becomes $n \times m$:

$$FV = PV \times \left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^{n \times m}$$

---

## 3. Annuities & Perpetuities

### Ordinary Annuity vs. Annuity Due
An **annuity** is a sequence of equal cash flows occurring at equal intervals.

1. **Ordinary Annuity**: Cash flows occur at the **END** of each period (e.g., standard bond coupon payments).
   $$PV_{\text{ordinary}} = PMT \times \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}{r} \right]$$

2. **Annuity Due**: Cash flows occur at the **BEGINNING** of each period (e.g., apartment lease rent payments). Cash flows earn 1 additional period of interest:
   $$PV_{\text{due}} = PV_{\text{ordinary}} \times (1 + r)$$

### Perpetuity
A **perpetuity** is a continuous stream of equal cash flows that continues forever.

$$PV_{\text{perpetuity}} = \frac{PMT}{r}$$

---

## 4. Practical Financial Application

Suppose an investor deposits **$10,000** today into an account yielding an **8% annual return**, compounded annually for **5 years**.

- $PV = \$10,000$
- $r = 0.08$
- $n = 5$

$$FV = 10,000 \times (1 + 0.08)^5 = 10,000 \times 1.4693 = \$14,693.28$$

The investor earns **$4,693.28** in cumulative compound interest over 5 years.
