A **payoff diagram** is a visual representation of the net profit or loss generated by a derivative position at contract expiry across a range of potential underlying asset prices. Understanding payoff charts enables traders and risk managers to evaluate potential gains, losses, and break-even points before entering a trade.

## 1. What a Payoff Diagram Represents

In a standard derivative payoff chart:
- The **Horizontal Axis ($X$-axis)** represents the prevailing spot price of the underlying asset at expiration ($S_T$).
- The **Vertical Axis ($Y$-axis)** represents the net profit or loss ($\text{P\&L}$) per contract.
- The **Origin ($Y = 0$)** represents the **Break-Even Line**, dividing profitable outcomes from loss-making outcomes.

## 2. Why Futures Payoffs are Linear and Symmetric

Unlike options—which provide the buyer with a choice and produce non-linear, asymmetric payoffs—a futures contract creates a **binding legal obligation** on both the buyer and seller.

Because both parties are committed to fulfilling the contract regardless of market moves:
- **Linear Profile**: For every \$1 move in the underlying asset's price, the profit or loss moves by an exact, constant amount.
- **Symmetric Profile**: Gains and losses are mirror images of each other. The dollar gain of the long position is identical to the dollar loss of the short position at any given expiration price.

## 3. Long Futures Position Payoff

A **long futures position** is established by a buyer who expects the underlying asset's price to rise. 

### Formula
$$
\text{P\&L}_{\text{Long}} = (S_T - K) \times L
$$

*Where $S_T$ is the spot price at expiration, $K$ is the contracted futures price, and $L$ is the lot size.*

### Key Characteristics
- **Profit Condition**: Generates profit when $S_T > K$.
- **Loss Condition**: Incurs a loss when $S_T < K$.
- **Upside & Downside**: Theoretically unlimited profit upside as $S_T$ increases, while maximum downside loss is bounded only by the underlying price falling to zero ($S_T = 0$).

### Worked Example
Suppose an investor enters a long stock futures contract:
- **Contracted Futures Price ($K$)**: \$500
- **Lot Size ($L$)**: 100 shares

**Scenario 1 ($S_T = \$540$)**:
$$
\text{P\&L} = (\$540 - \$500) \times 100 = +\$4,000 \text{ profit}
$$

**Scenario 2 ($S_T = \$460$)**:
$$
\text{P\&L} = (\$460 - \$500) \times 100 = -\$4,000 \text{ loss}
$$

## 4. Short Futures Position Payoff

A **short futures position** is established by a seller who expects the underlying asset's price to fall.

### Formula
$$
\text{P\&L}_{\text{Short}} = (K - S_T) \times L
$$

### Key Characteristics
- **Profit Condition**: Generates profit when $S_T < K$.
- **Loss Condition**: Incurs a loss when $S_T > K$.
- **Upside & Downside**: Profit is capped at $K \times L$ (if the underlying asset drops to zero), while downside loss is theoretically unlimited as $S_T$ can rise indefinitely.

### Worked Example
Using the same parameters ($K = \$500$, $L = 100$ shares):

**Scenario 1 ($S_T = \$460$)**:
$$
\text{P\&L} = (\$500 - \$460) \times 100 = +\$4,000 \text{ profit}
$$

**Scenario 2 ($S_T = \$540$)**:
$$
\text{P\&L} = (\$500 - \$540) \times 100 = -\$4,000 \text{ loss}
$$

## 5. Break-Even Analysis: Futures vs. Options

A crucial advantage in reading futures payoff charts is calculating the **Break-Even Point (BEP)**:

$$
\text{Break-Even Price } (S_T) = K
$$

Because entering a futures contract requires **zero upfront premium** (only collateral margin deposits, which are fully refundable), the break-even price is simply the contracted futures price itself:
- In **Futures**: Break-even occurs exactly at $S_T = K$.
- In **Options**: Break-even requires the price to move beyond the strike price by the amount of option premium paid (e.g., $S_T = \text{Strike} + \text{Premium}$).

## 6. Practical Interpretation & Risk Management

Traders use futures payoff diagrams to analyze their risk-return ratio before executing trades:

1. **Direct Price Exposure**: Because a futures payoff has no downside cap, holding an unhedged (naked) futures position exposes the trader to 1-to-1 market risk.
2. **Margin Management**: Reading the payoff slope helps traders compute how far the underlying price can move adversely before triggering a margin call.
3. **Zero-Sum Game**: The long and short payoff charts are exact inverses. Across the market, every dollar gained by a long futures holder is matched by a dollar lost by a short futures holder.
